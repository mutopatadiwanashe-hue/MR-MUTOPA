BANKGUARD AI COMPLIANCE KNOWLEDGE BASE

RULE 1: CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE AND KYC
Banks should verify customer identity and maintain appropriate customer information before providing financial services. Transactions involving customers whose KYC information is incomplete or unverified should receive additional compliance attention.

RULE 2: LARGE TRANSACTIONS
Large-value transactions require enhanced monitoring because they may present increased money laundering or financial crime risks. BankGuard AI should flag unusually large transactions for additional assessment.

RULE 3: TRANSACTION FREQUENCY
A high frequency of transactions within a short period may indicate unusual activity. Transactions with unusually high frequency should be monitored and may require compliance review.

RULE 4: INTERNATIONAL TRANSACTIONS
International transactions may require additional monitoring because cross-border activity can create increased compliance and money laundering risks.

RULE 5: BUSINESS-TO-PERSONAL TRANSFERS
Transfers from business accounts to personal accounts should receive additional scrutiny where the transaction does not have a clear legitimate purpose. BankGuard AI may require administrator authorization for such transactions.

RULE 6: HIGH-RISK TRANSACTIONS
Transactions classified as high risk should be blocked according to BankGuard AI security rules and may require administrator authorization before further action.

RULE 7: MEDIUM-RISK TRANSACTIONS
Transactions classified as medium risk should be placed under compliance review before being approved.

RULE 8: AUDIT TRAIL
Security decisions should be recorded in an audit trail containing information such as transaction ID, risk level, decision, reasons, flags and timestamp.

RULE 9: HUMAN COMPLIANCE REVIEW
Automated systems should support compliance officers rather than completely replacing human oversight. Transactions requiring review should be presented to an authorized compliance reviewer.

RULE 10: SECURITY PRINCIPLE
BankGuard AI should use multiple security signals, including fraud risk, KYC status, AML indicators, transaction monitoring and account controls, when determining the overall transaction risk.
